Built for outdoor use.

Built for outdoor use.

Trail Mate implements basic Meshtastic and MeshCore communication so you can contact nearby compatible terminals to exchange information, ask for help, or coordinate outdoors.

This is selective protocol support, not a complete implementation or feature parity with either ecosystem's native firmware. Advanced radio features unrelated to outdoor use are outside Trail Mate's scope. Protocol work is guided by practical field needs.

01 / IDENTITY

Identity in your hands.

Device identity can exist independently of cloud accounts and phone numbers. Keep your contacts and conversations on personally controlled hardware.

02 / CONNECTION

Choose your network.

Select Meshtastic, MeshCore or Reticulum. One protocol is active at a time. Meshtastic and MeshCore support focuses on basic outdoor communication, rather than complete protocol feature coverage.

03 / LOCAL RESOURCES

Prepare maps and routes.

Keep maps, contacts and recorded tracks on the device. Prepare the resources you need before departure; desktop tools can help manage maps and routes.

04 / TEAM AWARENESS

Know where your group is.

On-device TAK brings together members, positions, waypoints, tracks and team messages. Public discovery, contact positions, team positions and local tracks remain distinct.

Built for days when a phone is not enough: keep maps on SD, send messages without mobile networks, record tracks, check signal and GPS, and install extra language packs when you need them.

WHY A SCIENTIFIC CALCULATOR?

Calculations for field surveying.

Calculations for field surveying.

The scientific calculator supports outdoor surveying: estimate height differences, convert slope distances to horizontal distances, and calculate slope angles or gradients from your measurements.

01 / HEIGHT DIFFERENCE

Estimate a height.

At a horizontal distance of 30 m and an elevation angle of 35°, the height above the observation level is 30 × tan(35°) ≈ 21.01 m.

For tree height, add instrument height when the tree base and your footing are level; otherwise account for the ground elevation difference.

02 / DISTANCE COMPONENTS

Convert slope distance.

A straight 100 m segment at 20° has a horizontal distance of 100 × cos(20°) ≈ 93.97 m and a rise of 100 × sin(20°) ≈ 34.20 m.

Calculate curved routes or changing slopes segment by segment.

03 / SLOPE

Calculate the gradient.

A 12 m rise over a 40 m horizontal run gives atan(12 ÷ 40) ≈ 16.70°, or a 30% grade. Degrees and percent grade are different units.

Supports sin, cos, tan, inverse trigonometric functions, powers, roots and logarithms, with DEG/RAD modes. Enter measured angles and distances; the calculator does not measure them.
