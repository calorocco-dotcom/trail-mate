## Decentralization and privacy

Users control their device identity and local records without handing their
complete private trail history to one account platform. The browser uses the
device's author identity over USB, while the computer carries Reticulum network
requests. Changing the device's chat/RF protocol is not required. Independent
operators can provide directories and gateways, and recipients can verify
author signatures independently.

This reduces the need for one platform to collect and correlate everyone's
activity, but does not guarantee that public information cannot be retained or
analysed. Published routes, coordinates, timestamps, public keys and metadata
are visible to directories and recipients. Publications under the same author
can be linked. Gateways and external map providers can observe connection or
map-request metadata. Archiving changes publication state; it cannot withdraw
historical copies already held by others.

The README and Wiki must not claim that no platform can ever learn a user's
trails or behaviour. Explain which data stays local, what users choose to share,
and what recipients can see.
